# TRI — resolving what the round surfaced

Tri is where things get resolved, not just written down. Whatever
surfaced during this round — a halting defect, a review finding worth
revisiting, a stashed thought — gets tackled head-on here, out loud with
Sam, until each one has landed somewhere real.

## The halt

If any halting defect is open, tri is the only permitted step — nothing
else in the cycle proceeds until it's cleared. Say this plainly and
don't offer a menu of other options: no "acknowledge and move on," no
guessing at a quick fix and proceeding anyway. Until it's resolved,
every other phase stays blocked, no exceptions.

## The resolution protocol, out loud

For each open item, walk through the same four questions with Sam, in
order:

1. **Fix at the source.** What's actually wrong, and where does the
  wrongness originate — a document, a decision, an assumption that
  stopped holding? Fix that, not just its downstream symptom.
2. **Propagate to docs.** Does the fix mean anything else written down
  needs to change to match? Find it and change it, out loud, rather than
  leaving the docs to quietly disagree with the fix.
3. **Propagate to the backlog.** Does any planned work — a PBI, an epic
  — need to change because of this fix? Same treatment: find it, change
  it.
4. **Disposition.** Does this resolution belong to this project alone,
  to the wag process itself, or both? A project-only fix stays local.
  Anything that should change how every project works needs to be
  embedded into the process itself, immediately — never left as
  something to remember later.

Don't move to the next open item until all four questions have been
answered out loud for the current one.

## What approve does here

Once every halting defect is cleared, every review finding from this
round has a disposition, and the stash has at least been offered for
triage, approve closes the round: it records the resolutions and clears
the way for the next design conversation to start clean. Restate what's
being closed out and get an explicit yes before calling it.
